## Usage

Tour walks the user through parts of the page one step at a time, dimming everything but the current target and
showing a tooltip next to it. Keep `Tour.Model` (from `Tour.init({ id })`) in your model, send `Tour.Message.Started()`
(or `StartedAt({ step })`) to begin, and pass its messages through
`Tour.update(model, message, Tour.tourContext({ steps, ...options }))`. Each step is
`{ target, title, children }`, where `target` is the id of the element it points at.

<Configurator story="overlays-tour--configurator" />

## Beacon mode

With `mode: "beacon"`, starting the tour shows a pulsing beacon on each target instead of the tooltip. Clicking a
beacon opens its step; closing the step goes back to the beacons, and `Tour.Message.Ended()` turns the tour off.
`color` sets the beacons and the Next button.

<Example height={260} story="overlays-tour--beacon" caption="Show the beacons, then click one" />

## Controlled

The model keeps the step. `StartedAt({ step })` opens the tour at a given step, `SetStep({ step })` moves it, and
the out messages report what happened: `StepChanged`, `StepOpened`, `StepClosed` and `Closed`.

<Example height={260} story="overlays-tour--controlled" caption="Jump straight to step 2" />

## Overlay configuration

`overlayColor` (`rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.5)` by default), `spotlightPadding` (8) and `spotlightRadius` (4) shape the dimmed
overlay and its cut-out. `withOverlayInteraction` lets clicks reach the target through the cut-out while the rest
of the overlay keeps blocking them, and `closeOnOverlayClick` closes the tour when the overlay is clicked. Steps can
override `withOverlay`, `spotlightPadding` and `spotlightRadius`.

<Example height={260} story="overlays-tour--overlay" caption="Navy overlay, wide rounded spotlight, click-through" />

## Steps without target

A step without a `target` is centered on the screen with no spotlight.

<Example height={200} story="overlays-tour--centered" caption="Centered first and last steps" />

## Tooltip position

`position` on a step places the tooltip around its target, `bottom` by default; it flips to the other side when
there is no room.

<Example height={280} story="overlays-tour--position" caption="Bottom, top, left and right" />

## Custom content

A step's `children` can be anything. To lay out the tooltip yourself, give `renderTooltip`: it receives the built
parts (`closeButton` and `title`, `null` when there are none, `body`, `navigation`) with the `step` and its `index`,
and returns the tooltip's content in your order, with anything of your own around them:

<Example height={260} story="overlays-tour--custom-content" caption="An icon above each title" />

## Transitions

The tooltip pops in over 200ms; `transitionProps` (`transition` by name, `duration`, `timingFunction`) changes it.
Between steps the same tooltip slides to the next target over `stepTransitionDuration` (300ms) instead of appearing
again.

## Scrolling and portal

A target outside the viewport is scrolled into view, centred and instantly; `scrollIntoViewOptions` (`behavior`,
`block`) changes how, and `withScrollIntoView: false` turns it off. The tour renders in a portal at the end of the
body unless `withinPortal: false`.

## Custom labels

`labels` replaces the `skip`, `back`, `next` and `close` button labels, the `stepCounter` text and the `beacon`
label.

<Example height={260} story="overlays-tour--labels" caption="Spanish labels" />

## Styles API

Tour's named parts are `root`, `overlay`, `spotlight`, `tooltip`, `title`, `body`, `navigation`, `closeButton`,
`stepCounter`, `beacon`, `beaconPulse` and `navigationButton`. `zIndex` (10000), `maxWidth` (360), `tooltipShadow`
(`md`), `withCloseButton`, `withScrollIntoView` and the keyboard options are props too.

<Example height={260} story="overlays-tour--states" caption="No overlay, no close button, narrow, grape" />

## Keyboard navigation

In guided mode Arrow Right goes to the next step (closing the tour on the last one) and Arrow Left to the previous
one, unless focus is inside the target or a form field, or `withKeyboardNavigation: false`. Escape closes the tour
unless `closeOnEscape: false`.

## Accessibility

The tooltip has `role="dialog"`, labelled by the step title (or the step counter) and described by the step body.
Focus moves into the tooltip when it opens and is trapped there while the overlay is shown, and returns to the
element that had it when the tour closes. The step counter is
announced with `aria-live="polite"`, and beacons are buttons named by `labels.beacon` and the step title, for example
`Start tour: Settings`.

## Differences from Mantine

- `target` is an element id rather than a CSS selector or a ref, and steps are plain values in `steps`.
- State lives in your model: `active` is `Started`/`Ended`, and `onStepChange`, `onStepOpen`, `onStepClose` and
  `onClose` are out messages; per-step `onStepOpen` and `onStepClose` are the same messages with the step index.
- Custom layouts use `renderTooltip` with the built parts instead of the compound components (`Tour.Root`,
  `Tour.Overlay`, `Tour.Tooltip` and the rest), so the tour keeps its own state.
- `scrollToHandler` is `scrollIntoViewOptions`: a function cannot be passed to the scroll handling, so you choose the
  behaviour and alignment instead.
- The tooltip animates in but not out: closing the tour removes it at once.
